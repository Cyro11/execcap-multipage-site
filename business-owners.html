The owner's question

Who will carry the business when you step back?

You may not be ready to leave tomorrow. You may not want a financial buyer who changes the character of the company. An operator-led transition creates space to think about the next chapter before a sale process dictates it for you.

The right successor does not simply purchase what you built. They understand what must be protected-and what must evolve.
What is at stake

Your team should not learn the future from a closing announcement.

Employees and customers experience a transaction as a change in leadership. Early transition planning makes continuity part of the value conversation.

What fit means

Relevant operating judgment matters more than a generic buyer profile.

The strongest successor is someone whose experience, temperament, and motivation match the business you actually run.

What timing means

Good handoffs are designed before they are urgent.

Clarify your desired role, the transition period, customer relationships, and decision rights while you still have room to choose.

What capital means

The transaction path should serve the business-not the other way around.

Capital structure, governance, and successor readiness should be discussed alongside the company, not after a buyer is already committed.

A thoughtful handoff

Make the transition visible before it becomes the risk.

ExecCap helps bring owner priorities, operator fit, and transaction readiness into one conversation so that the successor can step into a business with context-not just keys.

A private first step
