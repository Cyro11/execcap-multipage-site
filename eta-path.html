The ExecCap ownership path

A roadmap with real decision points.

Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition is not a checklist. It is a set of choices about the person who will lead, the business worth buying, and the capital structure that lets both work.

Discuss your starting point ->
Start with the person

Before you search for a company, define the owner you intend to be.

You may have the operating experience. The work is translating it into a credible ownership thesis: what you can lead, what you will not compromise, and what kind of business can reward that judgment.

"The acquisition only works if the future operator has a credible mandate to lead it."
The tension

You want ownership, but not a blind leap.

Starting from zero is not the only route to entrepreneurship. Buying an established business can create a faster path-but only when the role, risk, and target fit the operator.

The discipline

Every stage should produce a reason to proceed.

A target is not progress by itself. A strong thesis gives you criteria, a sourcing plan, and permission to walk away when a deal asks you to become someone you are not.

The journey

Follow the questions that make ownership real.

Scroll through the five decisions. The active marker shows where the work is happening; the output at each stage gives the next conversation something concrete to build on.

01 - Define the ownership thesis

What kind of owner are you trying to become?

Clarify your operating strengths, ownership goals, risk boundaries, preferred industries, and the leadership situations where you create the most value. This is where "I want to buy a business" becomes a thesis you can explain to a seller or capital partner.

Output: a written ownership thesis and non-negotiables
02 - Build the target map

Which businesses give that thesis room to work?

Translate the thesis into target criteria, market priorities, size parameters, and a sourcing approach. A good target map narrows the field without pretending the perfect company will arrive fully formed.

Output: target criteria, sourcing lanes, and a screening scorecard
03 - Evaluate business and transition

What will you actually inherit on day one?

Look past the income statement. Assess customers, employees, operating cadence, concentration, owner dependence, and the handoff required to move from seller-led to operator-led.

Output: a business-and-transition view that can survive diligence
04 - Structure capital and alignment

Can the capital structure support the operator?

Discuss financing, governance, economics, control, and decision rights in the same room. Capital should give the operator a credible mandate, not quietly replace it with a term sheet.

Output: a financing and alignment path with roles clearly stated
05 - Close with an operating plan

What happens in the first 100 days?

Prepare the transition message, leadership cadence, customer and employee priorities, and early value-creation agenda before the purchase agreement is the only thing left to discuss.

Output: a first-100-days agenda tied to the business you bought
Your starting point

Bring the question you are actually asking.

You do not need to have a target selected to begin. The first conversation can start with your operating background, your ownership goals, or the uncertainty that is holding you back.
