Ownership transition

The first 100 days begin before close.

Identify the relationships and operating priorities that matter on day one.

ExecCap Advisors · Field note

In this note

RelationshipsOperating rhythmSeller handoffFirst priorities

The closing date changes ownership. It does not automatically transfer relationships, operating knowledge, or trust. Transition planning deserves attention while you still have time to ask questions and learn from the seller.

Map the relationships.

Identify the employees, customers, suppliers, and other partners who will be important in the first months. Understand how those relationships are maintained and who carries the knowledge behind them.

Understand the operating rhythm.

Learn how the business makes decisions, monitors performance, resolves issues, and communicates. The existing rhythm can reveal both strengths to preserve and gaps that need attention.

Plan the seller handoff.

Discuss responsibilities, availability, introductions, and the knowledge that needs to transfer. A clear handoff gives both parties a more useful expectation of the transition.

Prioritize the first changes.

Decide what needs immediate attention and what should wait until you understand the business better. Early listening and clear communication can be as consequential as a new system or initiative.

Four questions for the transition plan

Who needs to hear from the new owner? What needs to continue without interruption? What knowledge must transfer? Which decisions need an early owner?

The first-100-days plan should connect to the acquisition thesis. It is a practical explanation of how ownership will begin and how the team will learn what to do next.
