Capital should follow operating conviction

See the whole picture before you size the check.

ExecCap helps frame the questions around operator readiness, business quality, transition risk, capital structure, and post-close governance before an acquisition becomes a financing exercise.

Operator readiness

Leadership capacity, decision-making context, relevant operating experience, and commitment to leading after close.

What ExecCap brings: a clearer ownership thesis and a candid view of where the operator is prepared-or still developing.

Transaction fit

Business durability, owner dependence, customer concentration, transition complexity, and the diligence path the target deserves.

What ExecCap brings: the operator's conviction connected to the company's actual operating conditions.

Alignment & governance

Scope, control, economics, decision rights, reporting cadence, and the places where incentives could quietly diverge.

What ExecCap brings: plain-English alignment questions before they become structural surprises.

Post-close execution

The seller handoff, first 100 days, team communication, and value-creation work that turn a transaction into an operating company.

What ExecCap brings: a plan that treats close as the beginning of the operating relationship.

Plain-English diligence

Before an engagement, ask the questions that protect alignment.

Scope & roles

Who is doing what work, when, and with what decision rights?

Clarity about the advisory role, the operator's responsibility, and the capital partner's involvement keeps the relationship workable after the term sheet.

Economic relationships

Are the incentives and material relationships visible?

Potential conflicts should be identified and discussed directly so that the structure supports trust instead of asking people to infer it.
